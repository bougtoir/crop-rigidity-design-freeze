# 01 — Novelty audit

Question searched: has a global/subnational study tested
*pre-existing locally fixed agricultural capital × climatic mismatch →
subsequent local crop reallocation*?

## What exists

- **Spatial reallocation is documented** (Sloat et al. 2020, Nat Commun):
  harvested-area migration of wheat/maize/rice/soybean 1973–2012 already
  moderated exposure to warming — the strongest evidence that H4's
  reallocation channel is real.
- **Counterfactual reallocation is modelled** (Rising & Devineni 2020 US;
  China 2023): optimization shows large gains from crop switching — but
  outcomes are simulated, not observed transformation.
- **Fixed-capital mechanisms are established case-wise**: irrigation
  institutional lock-in (Libecap), sunk-cost adoption hysteresis
  (Carey & Zilberman), perennial real-options replacement costs
  (orchard/macadamia literature), irrigation "trap" modelling.
- **Abandonment is observable** in regional studies (Zambia provinces,
  China cropland) but not conditioned on fixed capital globally.

## Gap

No global or multi-region empirical study links *pre-committed,
spatially fixed* capital (irrigation share, perennial share, persistent
crop geography) × climatic mismatch to *observed* local transformation
outcomes (switching, abandonment, entry) while separating within- vs
between-location reallocation. Existing work is either (a) reallocation
itself without conditioning, (b) counterfactual optimization, or
(c) case-level fixed-capital mechanism evidence.

The proposed design — especially H4 (aggregation escape), with the
within/between decomposition — is novel relative to `01_NOVELTY_MATRIX.csv`.

## Risk to novelty

Sloat et al. already show the *existence* of the escape channel. Novelty
rests on the **conditioning** claim (fixed capital predicts *who* fails to
transform) and the **decomposition** (national flexibility ≠ local
flexibility), not on documenting reallocation.
